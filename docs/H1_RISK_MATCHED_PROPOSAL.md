# H1 risk-matched study and prospective freeze proposal

Status: written proposal only, September 23, 2026. No approval, registration, implementation, new outcome calculation, backtest, broker action or subscription follows from this document. Existing code and risk limits remain unchanged. This proposes one H1 study; it does not reopen H2–H4 or the failed MR/MOM families. Choose one sizing and halt policy before any additional outcomes are inspected; do not run the options and select whichever looks best.

## Interpretation of existing evidence

Dividing profit by average exposure is a useful description, not an executable leveraged return or an equal-risk comparison. It ignores the timing of exposure, covariance, compounding, costs and the passive sleeve's drift. The lower stop-risk cap materially constrains H1's allocation, but signals, asset selection and exit timing still affect its outcome. The existing evidence establishes neither independent alpha nor its absence. The GLD concentration and small number of closed episodes remain concerns.

The old experiment failed its frozen primary hurdle. The new comparison is a new, explicitly approved research question; it cannot turn that failure into a retrospective pass. All outcomes through September 23, 2026 are consumed history. Historical validation of any approved adaptation remains development, not confirmation.

## 1. One primary matched benchmark and paired test

**Proposed primary question:** does H1 produce positive net daily excess return over an equal-weight five-ETF basket scaled to H1's estimated portfolio volatility using only information available at the decision close? This controls estimated risk magnitude while leaving composition and its consequences to be compared. It is not a test of H1's entire risk-timing benefit against a completely independent passive policy, because the control deliberately inherits H1's risk path. Even a pass would support incremental performance against this specified control, not prove market-neutral alpha or eliminate different asset/factor exposures.

Freeze the H1 signal: month-end total-return close above its 200-session mean, existing trend exit, signal-close stop at three ATR20 below the raw signal close, completed-close protective checks, next-open execution, fixed universe SPY/QQQ/IWM/TLT/GLD. Freeze one sizing and halt option from the next sections separately. No signal tuning or new assets.

### Causal benchmark construction

At each completed session close t, after the required data are actually available:

1. Estimate covariance C_t from the most recent 60 complete, aligned daily simple total returns for all five ETFs, including t, using sample covariance (ddof=1). The first estimation has 61 closes. Use no observations after t, full-window fitted volatility, forward-filled prices or hindsight weight normalization.
2. Let w_t be H1's **actual closing dollar holdings divided by closing equity**. Cash and unpaid receivables have zero risky weight. Let u be a fixed vector of five 0.20 weights. Annualized estimated volatility is sqrt(252 × w_t' C_t w_t) for H1 and sqrt(252 × u' C_t u) for the full basket.
3. Set the benchmark's desired risky fraction a_t = min(S, estimated_H1_vol / estimated_basket_vol), where S is the approved H1 sleeve maximum. Its target ETF weights are a_t × u. Apply the same approved per-name and equity-cluster exposure ceilings; leave clipped capacity in cash without redistribution. A cap can prevent matching: retain and report the predicted-volatility ratio and exposure tracking error rather than claim exact matching.
4. Plan whole-share target quantities from the benchmark's own equity and raw closes at t; execute at the next actual session open, sells before buys in fixed alphabetical order. Quantities may reduce at execution for cash and exposure constraints, never increase using the opening price. Costs and dividends affect the benchmark's own ledger, not a copied H1 balance. The existing overnight holding earns its actual close-to-open return before the rebalance; no synthetic assignment of today's exposure to today's entire return.
5. Rebalance this control daily to its frozen target rule and charge its actual turnover. Do not omit control costs or choose a new rebalance frequency after seeing results. Report turnover and a gross paired diagnostic so any advantage attributable to frequent control rebalancing is visible. The benchmark has no H1 trend filter, stop or portfolio-liquidation rule: those would change the comparator. A H1 halt reduces its future risk target through observed H1 weights with the stated lag; it does not retroactively liquidate the benchmark.

The primary targets **ex-ante estimated** volatility, not realized volatility. Proposed matching-validity gate: on at least 95% of sessions with positive H1 estimated volatility, the clipped benchmark target must have predicted volatility within 10% of H1's estimate under the same covariance. Joint zero-risk sessions are reported separately. If this gate fails, retain the paired results as descriptive control performance but label the equal-risk claim inconclusive; do not raise the control sleeve or rescale returns afterward. Report realized-volatility ratios too, without using them to refit the comparator. Allocation differences from overnight gaps, next-open fills and the one-session information lag remain visible. Never scale the whole benchmark after observing the confirmation period to force equal realized volatility.

**Secondary, descriptive control:** use a_t = min(S, sum(w_t)), with the same daily next-open construction, accounting and costs. This is lagged exposure matching. Retain the original fully invested and initially 25%-invested static baskets as context; neither becomes a replacement primary after outcomes. No independent success claim from choosing whichever control gives the largest excess.

If H1 has zero exposure, the next target is zero. If the covariance is invalid, basket volatility is zero, or any required history is missing, block new benchmark exposure and mark the intended match unavailable. Do not invent an allocation. Maintain existing ownership and genuinely available marks, record unmatched days, and treat unresolved paired valuation/data gaps as inconclusive rather than deleting observations. H1's strategy data rules continue independently.

### Startup and accounting

Initialize H1 and each comparator with a separate $100,000 cash balance on October 1, 2026, no legacy positions, receivables or external cash flows. Freeze the rules before September 30 close; use that completed close to queue H1's first possible October 1 entry. For the benchmark's **startup only**, use H1's planned post-entry close-valued weights from that same September 30 decision, since there are no actual H1 holdings yet. Thereafter use actual closing weights as above. Any gap-related mismatch in the first executions is recorded, not repaired with hindsight. No September performance is credited.

Proposed signal-data cutoff: 09:20 America/New_York on the first actual session following the signal close, before its scheduled 09:30 regular-session open. The extra-session execution diagnostic does not extend this information-eligibility deadline; its target is already fixed before the additional delay. Required completed-bar and action inputs must actually have arrived by that cutoff; save their arrival times and the real decision timestamp rather than label a late calculation as occurring at the prior close. No overnight price observation enters the close-based signal. Later revisions are retained for audit but cannot retroactively change a queued target or fill. Missing required entry inputs skip that opening entry/rebalance and wait for the next scheduled eligible signal; missing exit inputs preserve economic ownership and pending exit state without inventing a fill. This exact cutoff is part of the proposal requiring approval and availability verification, including startup. Early closes affect the prior session's completion time; the actual exchange schedule remains authoritative.

Base modeled execution drag is 7 bps per side, stress 14 bps. An adverse-timing diagnostic adds one actual session to planned transactions for both H1 and its matched control; preserve pending-order deadlines and prohibit later information from increasing a planned order. Construct each stress control from that scenario's H1 weights and retain it as a distinct diagnostic. The old static comparison convention remains unchanged in old artifacts. These are assumptions until independently calibrated.

For the delayed daily benchmark, queue one immutable absolute portfolio target per decision session, due at the specified later open. Later targets do not overwrite earlier targets. At each open process only the target due then, with sells before buys; recheck cash/exposure constraints and only reduce originally planned quantities. Expire missing-price entries/additions at their due open. Retain missing-price reductions as pending; a full exit has priority over a reduction, and a pending reduction blocks subsequent increases in that name until resolved. No later target can postpone a pending reduction. The comparator does not acquire H1's stops retroactively. H1 retains its own stronger protective/risk exits, cancels queued increases on a risk block, preserves an existing exit's earliest due time and never inherits an earlier deadline from a pending addition. These rules require implementation verification after approval; they are not a claim that the current adapter supports this new daily benchmark queue.

Use raw opens for execution, verified splits and cash distributions, unpaid dividend receivables until documented payment availability, zero cash interest, marked open positions at period end, and a separately labeled hypothetical liquidation value. Never force terminal trades into episode expectancy. Gross comparison uses the same transactions with execution drag added back; a zero-cost rerun is not equivalent. Define and freeze operating overhead before business-profit claims; do not infer zero overhead from a free data subscription. An unknown or asymmetric overhead burden cannot be assumed to cancel.

### Paired inference and decision

Primary daily observation d_t = H1 net close-to-close portfolio return minus matched-control net close-to-close portfolio return on the same actual session. Include cash days, halts, initial costs and open-position returns, and initialize the first return against $100,000. Report mean d_t, compounded terminal excess, exposure, realized/predicted volatility, drawdowns, turnover, asset contributions and closed-episode counts. Reduced drawdown is a separate descriptive benefit, not a substitute for positive mean excess.

Proposed confirmation period: October 1, 2026–September 30, 2027, exact independently verified exchange-session list frozen before registration. Assess efficacy once, after the final session and reconciliation. Require all scheduled sessions accounted for, no unresolved paired valuation gaps, at least 200 valid paired sessions and 12 complete calendar-month observations. Monthly observations are not assumed independent; a year may still have inadequate effective sample size.

Proposed inference: jointly resample aligned H1/control daily observations with a stationary block bootstrap, 100,000 resamples for each fixed mean block length 20 and 60 sessions, seed 20260923. For each block length form a one-sided 95% basic bootstrap lower bound on mean paired excess: sample mean minus the 95th percentile of centered bootstrap-mean deviations. Require both bounds above zero, positive base net trading P&L and positive terminal net excess. The two block lengths are conjunctive robustness conditions, not two chances to select significance. Require positive H1 net P&L and terminal matched excess under both fixed stress diagnostics too, without claiming independent additional significance tests. Propose retaining a maximum observed account drawdown of 3%, no persistent drawdown termination, and annualized traded notional no greater than 2.0 for H1 in each base/stress/delay path. Annualized turnover is buys plus sells at raw prices divided by mean daily equity, multiplied by 252/N. These gates apply to H1, not to the deliberately daily-rebalanced comparator; comparator drawdown and turnover are fully reported and its costs are charged. A different numerical risk gate requires explicit approval before registration, including if sizing option C is chosen. Retain all data/accounting requirements. Missing evidence or insufficient sample is inconclusive, not a pass.

This is one prospective claim for one preselected H1 policy. The previously inspected four-family search remains part of the research history. No early efficacy pass, optional stopping, best-asset exclusion, automatic extension, alternate benchmark rescue, or rule change after looking at outcomes. A safety stop does not erase subsequent cash days or permit restarting the experiment. No result authorizes trading by itself.

## 2. Research-only sizing choices — select one before evaluation

The alternatives below are **policy options, not approved settings or additional candidate runs**. H1's entry/trend rules remain fixed. B/C change its sizing and monthly rebalancing, so they are versioned H1 policy variants with their own freeze; their results cannot be pooled with original H1. No assertion that a chosen exposure is suitable for the user's money is made.

| Option | Gross/overnight ceiling | Name / equity-cluster ceiling | Modeled stop-risk ceiling: name / aggregate | Volatility target | Consequence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A: retain current H1 | 25% | 10% / 20% | 0.1% / 0.5% of account equity | None | Entry-only slot sizing; likely continued low exposure. Existing algorithm, not a meaningful-exposure solution by assumption. |
| B: proposed 25% sleeve variant | 25% | 10% / 20% | 0.5% / 1.5% | 3% annualized account volatility, upper target | Can use more of the existing sleeve; explicitly relaxes stop-risk budgets and adds monthly rebalancing. Requires user approval. |
| C: proposed larger sleeve variant | 50% | 20% / 40% | 1.0% / 3.0% | 6% annualized account volatility, upper target | Changes both exposure and stop-risk policy. Higher halt frequency and larger gap losses are plausible; no approval is implied. |

B is the narrower research option to consider if the purpose is testing whether the existing 25% sleeve can matter. It still changes risk policy. No option guarantees that its exposure or volatility target will be reached.

For B/C only, at each real month-end close, select ETFs that pass the unchanged H1 trend rule and are not blocked by cooldown, data failure or a pending risk exit. Use the same 60-return covariance history. Form positive inverse-individual-volatility weights v within this eligible set, normalized to sum to one. Target gross fraction g = min(S, target_account_vol / sqrt(252 × v' C_t v)); target name weights initially g × v. If there are no eligible assets, covariance is invalid, or an eligible asset has zero/nonfinite individual volatility, skip the allocation calculation and do not add exposure; do not silently drop that asset and renormalize the others. Apply the selected name/cluster ceilings, then fixed stop-risk and available-cash budgets, in a frozen proportional downscaling followed by alphabetical whole-share rounding; do not redistribute unused capacity to seek the target.

Rebalance continuing holdings at that monthly decision; this explicitly changes original H1's entry-only sizing. Hold weights between decisions except for existing protective/risk exits and mandatory exposure-cap reductions. New episodes keep the signal-close three-ATR20 stop; additions keep the existing episode stop and must fit the remaining position/aggregate stop-risk budgets, including execution drag. Reductions preserve proportional cost basis and episode accounting. Plan at closes and only downsize at opens. No intramonth volatility chasing or leverage. Daily realized volatility may exceed the target, and close-based stops cannot cap gap losses.

### Drawdown and halt interactions

Illustrative arithmetic, **not simulations or forecasts**: with fixed weights and a uniform 2% fall in all held risky assets, a 25% sleeve loses roughly 0.5% of account equity before costs. At a 50% sleeve, a 1% risky-asset fall does the same. A 12% sleeve decline at 25% exposure or 6% at 50% exposure corresponds approximately to 3% account loss. Actual paths, correlations, compounding, rebalancing, gaps and prior gains make these approximations unsuitable as drawdown bounds.

The 3% account drawdown termination and 0.5% daily-loss liquidation remain current settings unless the user separately approves a halt-policy change. Retaining them in B/C is an explicit option: more meaningful exposure can trigger them much more readily and prevent monthly signals from being held. Risk-to-stop sums are modeled distances, not maximum possible losses. Increasing a risk budget cannot justify linearly scaling the historical H1 return or drawdown.

## 4. H4 halt brittleness and monthly strategies

Existing evidence, not a new run: initial integer quantities differed slightly between zero-cost and base H4. On April 4, 2025 the daily losses were approximately −0.5181% and −0.4793%. Only the first crossed 0.5%, triggering broad next-open liquidation; the second retained more positions. This explains divergent later profit. Costs did not improve the same trades. See `DAILY_PATTERN_AUDIT.md` for the complete attribution.

An absolute daily account-loss trigger is defensible if the user's actual objective is to stop exposure after a specified account loss regardless of strategy horizon. It is a discontinuous policy by design, not an implementation error merely because two nearby paths diverge. However, a close-to-next-open liquidation cannot guarantee a 0.5% daily loss cap, and that threshold can dominate a monthly strategy at larger exposure. The current cutoff has not been justified as an economically appropriate threshold for this monthly mandate by these results.

Present these mutually exclusive halt options for user decision; do not implement or compare them now:

- **Retain current liquidation rule:** preserve the 0.5% trigger, next-open liquidation, one-full-session entry block and 3% persistent drawdown termination. Accept interruption of monthly holdings and possible threshold sensitivity.
- **Propose an entry-only daily pause:** keep the 0.5% trigger and block added risk for the next full session; existing positions retain their individual stops and monthly exits. Keep the 3% portfolio termination. This removes one cause of portfolio-wide turnover but explicitly accepts more continued loss exposure after a daily breach. It is a substantive risk relaxation, not a harmless fix.
- **Propose a different, independently justified liquidation threshold:** select a budget consistent with the chosen sleeve, anticipated volatility and the user's loss tolerance before any outcomes; do not set it to avoid the observed April 2025 loss. No numeric replacement is nominated from that incident. Removing the daily trigger entirely would require its own explicit decision.

Any hysteresis, multi-day trigger or volatility-scaled trigger would be another policy choice requiring a defined economic purpose and fresh registration. Do not add such rules merely to erase this observed threshold crossing. These H4 findings inform the risk-policy decision for H1; H4 remains outside the proposed new study.
